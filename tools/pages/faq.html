When CryoPaint works best

Cooling depends on sending heat straight to space. Ideal: clear blue sky, low humidity, still air, and a relatively flat, sky-facing surface with little or no insulation beneath. Under those conditions, expect up to 15°F below air temperature.

Clear view of sky

Critical

Do the YMCA "Y": raise your arms and look up. If you see blue sky between them, CryoPaint will work. Clouds, trees or even glass block the view of space. Maximum slope is 30 degrees, and the Y test works for angled surfaces too.

Low humidity

High

Ideal is under 30%. Performance drops gradually, then noticeably above about 60%, as water vapor blocks heat from escaping. In humid places expect 3 to 7°F below air rather than 15.

Still air

High

Wind drags the surface back toward air temperature, the way blowing on cold coffee warms it. Breezes and gusts are fine; sustained wind above 5 mph starts to cut performance. Corrugation ridges, parapets and wind shadows help keep a still layer of air on the surface.

Hot weather

Medium

The hotter it gets, the bigger the difference versus the alternatives.

Clean air

Low

Dust and pollution can block heat from escaping. Cleaner air, better cooling.

Day and night

Low

Works 24/7. Most noticeable on hot, sunny days, and keeps cooling at night under clear skies.

How much paint to use

One ounce per square foot is the minimum, not the target. Every gallon is the same paint; the rate you apply it at sets your cost per square foot, coat count, performance and lifespan.

Prices at the $250 gallon. Each extra ounce cuts absorbed sunlight by roughly 10 to 15% versus the coat beneath; going from 1 to 3 oz cuts it by about a quarter overall. More paint never means proportionally more degrees.

Build it up in coats

Never lay 2 or 3 oz down at once; a heavy wet coat cracks as it dries. Apply 1 oz / sq ft or less per coat, 30 to 45 minutes apart.

Mix very well

The special ingredients settle. Mix thoroughly before use, and don't thin it or add anything.
